How the bonus rules work

Egypt's Supreme Council of Universities applies a different weighting formula depending on the university type. Here's each one explained separately.

Government Universities

Minimum Exam 1 score: 1050 / 1600
  • GPA is added directly to the total (out of 40).
  • Exam 1 normally counts as: score × 60 ÷ 1600.
  • Exam 2 / subject score counts only if it reaches 1100 out of 1600, weighted as: score × 15 ÷ 1600.
Bonus rule If your Exam 1 score is 1090 or higher, the weight jumps from ×60 to ×69 — so the whole Exam 1 score is recalculated as score × 69 ÷ 1600, giving high scorers extra credit.

Private & National Universities

Minimum Exam 1 score: 800 / 1600
  • GPA is added directly to the total (out of 40).
  • Exam 1 normally counts as: score × 60 ÷ 1600.
  • Exam 2 / subject score counts if it reaches 900 out of 1600, weighted as: score × 15 ÷ 1600.
  • National (Ahliya) universities follow this same formula as private universities.
Bonus rule If your Exam 1 score is 1090 or higher, the weight jumps from ×60 to ×75 — the highest bonus of the three tracks, so private and national universities reward a strong Exam 1 score the most.

Higher Institutes

Minimum Exam 1 score: 890 / 1600
  • GPA is added directly to the total (out of 40).
  • Exam 1 normally counts as: score × 60 ÷ 1600.
  • Exam 2 / subject score counts if it reaches 900 out of 1600, weighted as: score × 15 ÷ 1600.
Bonus rule Same as government universities: if your Exam 1 score is 1090 or higher, the weight jumps from ×60 to ×69.

ACT conversion: Egyptian coordination scores ACT using Reading, English/Writing, and Math only. Your Exam 1 equivalent is the average of these three sections (out of 36), converted to a 1600-point scale using the official ACT–SAT concordance table.

Subject tests: Only one subject-test source counts, matching the exam you actually took — this applies the same way whether you selected SAT, EST, or ACT above. If you sat EST subject tests, enter the combined total (already out of 1600) in the EST 2 Subject tests field. If you sat ACT subject tests instead, enter each section score (out of 36) in the two ACT subject test fields — they're converted to an 800-point scale each and summed. Filling in one source locks the other automatically, so only one can ever be counted.

Basis of calculation: Total % = GPA (added as-is) + Exam 1 share [score × 60 ÷ 1600, or the bonus multiplier above if the score is 1090 or higher] + Exam 2 share [score × 15 ÷ 1600, only if the minimum threshold for the chosen university type is met]. EST follows the exact same formula as SAT.

This tool is an approximation based on the publicly announced rules of Egypt's Supreme Council of Universities. Minor details can change year to year, and each university may apply its own specific criteria, so please confirm with the university you're applying to if it's private or national, or with the official universities coordination office (Tansik) if it's government, before making any final decision.

A note on cohorts: this calculator reflects the formula currently published for students graduating in 2026–2027. The Ministry of Higher Education has not yet issued the equivalency rules for the 2027–2028 cohort — once those are announced, a separate, dedicated calculator will be built for that class rather than folded into this one.
